# Editorial Ink — honest review

| criterion | score | reason |
|---|---|---|
| First-glance story clarity | 4/5 | Street → map → street reads at once; the poster going from "every 10 min" to "5.0 → 9.1 min" lands the point. The meaning of the gauge (the loop unrolled) needs the speaker for one sentence. |
| World or template? | 4/5 | Every state is a scene with a fixed foreground (her, the shelter, the poster); no boxes-and-numbers slide. The map states are the most diagram-like of the five. |
| Character warmth / usefulness | 4/5 | Reads clearly per state (reading, checking her watch with hand on hip, arms up with the paper dropped, smiling and pointing). Her figure is stiff (straight ink legs) and the face is small from the back row. |
| Scaffold persistence | 5/5 | Route, both buses, gauge, character and poster are single elements; buses travel along the road, gauge marker slides, poses interpolate; one nameable change per click. |
| Legibility at distance | 4/5 | Tag 56 px, gauge values 54 px, loop labels 38–42 px, 9.1 at 200 px. Tick numbers (22 px) and the source line are small print by design. B/A badges touch at the 1-min gap. |
| Polish / craft | 4/5 | Ink wobble, pencil grain, hatching, paper texture, deterministic art. The bus front views are simple; the area under the poster (glass, bench) is vague. |
| Setting fit | 5/5 | Looks like a long-read explainer or a think-tank brief; the red pencil is natural for policy, legal and journalism audiences. |
| One-shot reliability | 3/5 | The kit makes it repeatable, but the character's joint angles and the layout around her gestures took three rounds; a new talk needs a render pass to catch overlaps. |
| PPTX feasibility | 3/5 | Text, gauge and stamp export natively; scenes become picture layers; ink-in and buses following the road's curve are approximated. |

## Three weakest things in the final renders
1. **Map states are quieter than the street states.** The park interior is mostly
   empty paper until state 3. With more time: an oblique (slightly tilted) aerial view
   with a few elevated buildings around the loop, so it stays an illustration rather than a plan.
2. **The character's body is stiff.** Straight legs and fixed stance; the arms carry all
   the acting. With more time: weight shift per pose (hip and leg angles), larger face
   features for the back row, a coat tail that swings during transitions.
3. **Gauge badges collide at the 1-minute gap** and the mid-transition pose interpolation
   passes through awkward in-between arm positions. With more time: tween poses through a
   neutral key pose, and let the B badge ride above the bar when a gap is under 2 minutes.
